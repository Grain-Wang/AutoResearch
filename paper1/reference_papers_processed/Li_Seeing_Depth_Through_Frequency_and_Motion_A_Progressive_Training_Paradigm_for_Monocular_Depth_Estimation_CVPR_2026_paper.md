# Seeing Depth Through Frequency and Motion: A Progressive Training Paradigm for Monocular Depth Estimation

## Abstract

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation has achieved remarkable progress in recent years, yet frequency aliasing and the lack of fine-grained cross-frame motion modeling still lead to blurred depth boundaries and suboptimal camera motion estimation. To address these challenges, we propose a progressive self-supervised framework that integrates a Frequency-Guided Depth Network (FGDepth) and a PoseQuery Network (PQNet). FGDepth incorporates a plug-and-play Frequency-Guided Sampling module that explicitly enhances high-frequency details and suppresses aliasing artifacts, producing depth maps with sharper boundaries. PQNet employs channel-aligned attention to model fine-grained cross-frame motion features, enabling more accurate and robust camera motion estimation. Furthermore, we design a progressive three-stage decoupled training strategy that effectively leverages the complementarity between depth and pose estimation, further improving overall performance. Extensive experiments on the KITTI benchmark demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, achieving a 4.1% reduction in Sq Rel over strong baselines, and our method also exhibits excellent crossdataset generalization on Make3D. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of each proposed component.

### 1. Introduction

Monocular depth estimation is a core task in 3D scene understanding. Owing to its hardware efficiency, requiring only a single camera to infer spatial geometry, it has become a research focus in fields such as autonomous driving and augmented reality. Self-supervised monocular depth estimation leverages unlabeled monocular videos with image reconstruction as a supervision signal, effectively avoiding the reliance on large-scale annotated datasets and offering great practical value [10, 39, 41]. In this paper, we aim to enhance self-supervised monocular depth estimation by explicitly addressing two critical limitations of existing methods. First, inspired by the Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem, we argue that edge blurring and structural degradation in monocular depth estimation originate from frequency aliasing introduced during the downsampling operations of encoder-decoder architectures. Second, while depth and pose networks are typically trained jointly, their complementary relationship-where accurate pose estimation can reinforce depth prediction and vice versa-remains largely underexploited, leaving the potential of leveraging reliable pose information for improving depth estimation insufficiently explored. It consists of two core components: (1) FGDepth, a frequency-aware depth network equipped with plug-and-play, architectureagnostic Frequency-Guided Sampling (FGS) blocks, which explicitly preserve high-frequency details via a high-pass enhancement path and suppress aliasing artifacts through a low-pass filtering path, thereby preserving the semantic integrity of low-frequency structures; and (2) PQNet with Channel-Aligned Attention, which captures fine-grained cross-frame correspondences for robust pose estimation. Furthermore, to fully leverage the complementarity between pose and depth estimation, we introduce a progressive three-stage training strategy, theoretically equivalent to Block Coordinate Descent, that decouples and gradually optimizes the two networks, significantly improving training stability and cross-dataset generalization. ### 2. Related Work

#### 2.1. Single-Stage Joint Optimization in MDE

In self-supervised monocular depth estimation, depth and camera ego-motion are jointly optimized in a single-stage framework from monocular videos, with supervision rooted in image reprojection loss [41]. Building on this methodology, prior work has advanced the field through key improvements. Refinements to loss functions have significantly improved the accuracy of depth estimation and mitigated challenges like scale ambiguity [10, 24]. Some approaches enhance depth prediction by leveraging auxiliary modalities, including optical flow and scene semantics [14, 20]. Recent efforts have been devoted to developing more elaborate architectural designs for enhanced depth estimation [26, 36, 38, 39]. Despite these improvements, unified joint training frameworks often struggle with depth ambiguity and limited generalization. These limitations can be primarily attributed to two factors: the aliasing of highfrequency information during feature extraction, and the inherent instability of single-stage joint training strategies.

#### 2.2. Frequency-Aware and Multi-Stage Training in

MDE Early methods relied on Fourier domain analysis to explicitly fuse multi-scale predictions [13]. Subsequently, spatial domain constraints, such as edge-aware smoothness losses guided by image gradients, were widely adopted to preserve structural boundaries [10]. More recently, researchers have sought to optimize the photometric loss in high-frequency regions from the perspective of spatial frequency [4]. Unlike computationally heavy global transforms, our method resolves aliasing via efficient spatial filtering, minimizing latency. Although single-stage joint optimization of depth and pose is a common strategy in MDE, its inherent complexity and instability have motivated multi-stage training approaches. These include two-stage schemes based on uncertainty modeling [19] or inter-frame consistency [18], and teacher-student architectures [21]. To further improve convergence, recent works also explore finer-grained, selfevolving progressive refinements [5, 15]. However, these approaches often overlook the integration of reliable motion cues, which is crucial for further improving depth accuracy.

#### 3.3. Progressive Three-Stage Training Strategy

We observe that more accurate pose estimation can enhance depth estimation performance, and vice versa, indicating that the two networks are capable of mutually reinforcing each other. Therefore, we propose a progressive three-stage training strategy that decouples the two networks during training and optimizes them under task-specific constraints at different stages, thereby fully exploiting their mutual interactions. #### 4.2. Implementation Details

The experiments were conducted on a 24-core Intel workstation equipped with 64GB of RAM and two NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs, each with 24GB of VRAM. The entire framework was implemented in PyTorch v1.9.0 , and all input images were resized to 640 × 192 before being fed into the network. To ensure fair comparison, the learning rate, number of epochs, batch size, and other training hyperparameters were kept consistent with the baseline [39].

#### 4.3. Evaluation Details

Since our method performs self-supervised depth estimation (predicting relative depth), we adopt the depth estimation metrics defined in previous work [6], including Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel), Squared Relative Error (Sq Rel), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Root Mean Squared Log Error (RMSElog), and accuracy within thresholds (δ < 1.251, 1.252, 1.253). For these metrics, lower values indicate Abs Rel, Sq Rel, RMSE, and RMSElog indicate better performance, while higher values for the δ metrics are preferred. It is worth noting that predicted depth is scaled to align with ground truth prior to evaluation, following standard practice [10]. For pose estimation, we use the 5-frame Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) as the evaluation metric, consistent with previous approaches [41].

### 5. Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a progressive self-supervised framework for monocular depth estimation, integrating FGDepth with Frequency-Guided Sampling (FGS) block to effectively recover high-frequency structural details while suppressing aliasing-induced artifacts, and a PoseQuery Network (PQNet) with channel-aligned attention to finegrained cross-frame motion cues. A three-stage decoupled training strategy fully leverages the complementarity between depth and pose estimation, further improving overall model performance. Extensive experiments on KITTI demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, including a 4.1% reduction in Sq Rel over strong baselines. Moreover, our findings reveal a mutually reinforcing relationship between depth and pose networks, suggesting that progressive, decoupled optimization may represent a promising paradigm for future self-supervised learning in this domain.
