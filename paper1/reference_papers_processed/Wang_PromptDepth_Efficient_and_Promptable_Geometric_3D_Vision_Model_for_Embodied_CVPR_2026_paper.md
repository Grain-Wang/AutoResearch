# PromptDepth: Efficient and Promptable Geometric 3D Vision Model for Embodied Intelligence

## Abstract

Vision models for embodied intelligence require efficient 3D comprehension and interaction with objects within the scene. Existing 3D reconstruction models either overlook instance-level perception or rely on time-consuming offline reasoning, showing a less adaptability in real-time embodied scenario. In this paper, we present PromptDepth, the first promptable vision model that features both geometric 3D understanding and instance-level interaction especially designed for embodied intelligence. PromptDepth is a feedforward network that quickly yields panoptic, instanced, or tracked depth map from two corresponding frames, enabling the real-time infer sequences from embodied agents. Specifically, following the minimal prediction problem, we design a promptable Dense Prediction Transformer, making it flexible to interact with unified dense prediction according to a specific prompt. Considering the substantial discrep- *Equal contribution. †Corresponding author. ancy between panoptic and instanced depth map, we further introduce a novel Instanced Label Distribution Smoothing (ILDS) loss, followed by Gram Anchoring, to mitigate the inherent conflict between dense and discrete representation. Trained on synthetic data only, our model achieves stateof-the-art results in both depth estimation and interactive segmentation on public benchmarks. Extensive experiments demonstrate superior visual efficiency in embodied tasks compared to current fundamental models. We believe that our efficient and flexible geometric 3D model offers a new foundation for vision tasks in embodied intelligence.

### 1. Introduction

Recently, embodied intelligence [4, 28, 39, 42] has attracted increasing attention in the computer vision. Considering the computational limitations of embodied platforms, it is crucial for their visual systems to efficiently understand 3D scenes and interact with objects within the visual field.

<!-- source-page: 2 -->

For instance, an embodied agent performing Go-to-Graspand-Place requires a series of visual tasks, including mapping, localization, instance recognition, and object tracking, within tolerable latency. In terms of 3D geometric understanding, existing visual models [20, 39, 42, 44] demonstrate superior performance in gathering 3D spatial information, like depth estimation, dense point cloud reconstruction, and 3D point tracking. However, most of them are less practical for embodied agents due to a lack of efficiency and interactivity. Specifically, recent advances [2, 9, 67] require the aggregation of sequential frames for a single forward pass, which is more suitable for offline inference rather than online applications. Besides, their multiple prediction heads for multi-tasks bring redundant inference; this paradigm is exactly contrary to the minimal prediction requirements of real-time systems. Aside from latency considerations, many fundamental models [17, 19, 29, 30, 36, 44, 69] fail to perceive instances beyond 3D geometry, which are naturally manipulatable objects within embodied visual systems. While simply combining 3D visual models [9, 76] with promptable visual models (e.g., SAM [32, 54]) is a possible strategy, this ”mixture-of-experts” approach inherently introduces significant latency and thus significantly spoils efficiency. Moreover, current diffusion-based [71,78] or gaussian-splatting [37, 64, 78] works advocate building instance-aware 3D maps for semantic interaction with the environment. However, neither the iterative process nor the implicit projection is run-time friendly. To this end, we present PromptDepth, an efficient and flexible vision model that features geometric 3D understanding and instance-level interaction, specially designed for embodied intelligence. Taking up to two frames as input, PromptDepth yields a corresponding dense prediction in an online manner. Existing fundamental vision models often employ redundant multi-head decoders for various tasks. We argue that this behavior results in overlapping functionality; for example, both a point map and a depth map contain geometric representations. Following the principle of minimal prediction, we propose a promptable Dense Prediction Transformer (promptDPT) that only focuses on geometric representation, leaving the specific prediction to be promptable, and thus adaptively produces panoptic, instanced, and tracked depth maps according to the specific prompt (Fig. 1 (b)). However, vanilla prompt engineering [32, 54] is not suitable for dense prediction. It struggles to handle the intricate interactions between visual embeddings and a series of sparse queries. Therefore, we disentangle this process using a cascaded two-way transformer consisting of a dense and a sparse component, with the former responsible for visual attention and the latter for sparse queries. The proposed PromptDepth is flexible enough to perform various embodied visual tasks, including depth estimation [13, 70], instance interaction [21, 40], and dense object tracking [51,73]. However, training our model for both instance-level and panoptic-level perception remains a challenging multi-task learning problem, because instanced depth labels—which are dominated by large backgrounds—may introduce biases into an already-imbalanced dense prediction task. Thus, we propose a novel Instanced Label Distribution Smoothing (ILDS) [77, 83], which adaptively generates balanced weights for both panoptic depth and instanced depth, providing a stable training scheme for such a complicated learning objective. In addition, another conflict between panoptic depth and instanced depth emerges in the latent space. A strong instance representation requires features of the same object to be similar; however, this can distort finegrained geometric correspondence, as features from geometrically distant parts of the same instance are forced together, resulting in a degradation of panoptic depth. Therefore, we further explore Gram Anchoring [62] to regularize the relationship between the geometric and instanced representations. With our proposed ILDS and Gram anchoring, our PromptDepth stop collapses during training and gain performance improvement. Due to the absence of geometric-instanced data pairs in the real world, we built our data engine within hundreds of dynamic synthetic environments to collect a large-scale, high-quality, and well-aligned synthetic dataset, as shown in Fig. 1 (a). Using synthetic data for training only, Prompt- Depth achieves state-of-the-art results on depth estimation and comparable instance segmentation and video object tracking tasks on public benchmarks, illustrating a robust zero-shot capability. Moreover, evaluations on embodied tasks show its significant advantage in visual efficiency over current 3D perception models. We believe that our dataset, code, and model will facilitate further research on vision models for embodied intelligence and benefit the computer vision and embodied intelligence communities. In all, our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We introduce PromptDepth, the first visual model for embodied intelligence that breaks down 3D scene comprehension into flexible instance-level interaction, capable of swiftly predicting various types of dense depth, whether for a scene, a queried instance, or a tracked object, according to a given prompt. • We built a realistic synthetic dataset covering more than 10M objects with well-aligned samples, empowering the visual community to perceive 3D instances. • Using synthetic data only, experiments showing our model perform robust zero-shot capability across diverse tasks, especially in limited latency scenario.

### 2. Related Work

Multi View Stereo aims to recover dense 3D geometry from posed images, fundamentally complementing Structure-from-Motion (SfM) [25, 41, 47, 59, 68], providing the necessary camera poses with corresponding sparse structure. It has rapidly advanced from traditional optimization methods—reliant on hand-crafted features [11, 12, 18, 23,58] and photometric consistency metrics [1,24,84]—to end-to-end data-driven learning methods [8, 26, 31, 34, 72, 79] by volume regularization or advanced transformer architectures. Crucially, the vast majority of current stateof-the-art MVS models [30, 67, 74] still inherit the off-line processing latency inherent in calculating dense geometry. While cutting-edge deep models pursue unified, real-time geometric prediction, the offline inference paradigm of contemporary MVS significantly limits its straightforward application in dynamic, low-latency environment [33,43,55]. In this regard, this work enables real-time capability by prioritizing high stereo accuracy between adjacent frames. Instance-aware Depth Estimation distinguishes itself from the holistic scene representation of MVS by achieving instance-level 3D reconstruction through the co-production of geometry and instantiated semantic descriptions [22,22, 71, 78]. Existing advanced approaches are primarily categorized into generative methods [27, 78, 82]—which learn 3D shape priors—and discriminative methods [5,19,35,38, 81] similar to panoptic depth estimation for semantic-togeometric or geometric-to-semantic guidance. From a latency perspective, the latter feed-forward approach is inherently more amenable to real-time inference scenarios. However, the simple combination of multi-task learning often proves less effective for exploring mutual promotion [75]. This difficulty is further compounded by the fact that building robust, open-world instanced geometric datasets is nontrivial, as they struggle with decomposing individual everything from synthetic environment [48, 56, 70, 80]. Driven by insights into the inherent feature disparities between geometric and class-agnostic instance descriptions, Prompt- Depth raise a seamless monocular panoptic depth estimation.

### 3. Method

As depicted in Fig. 2, the pipeline of our method consists of a symmetric visual encoder with entirely shared parameters, followed by a promptDPT decoder. Leveraging the cascaded two-way transformer within the promptDPT, the decoder can swiftly recognize all visual prompt it accept. We introduce the entire architecture of our model in Sec. 3.1, our designed promptDPT in Sec. 3.2, a novel loss design that addresses the imbalanced label distribution of dense prediction tasks in Sec. 3.3, and finally, the training setup derived from other learning objectives in Sec. 3.4.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

In this section, we introduce PromptDepth, a feedforward neural network that takes up to two corresponding images (I1, I2 ∈Rw×h×3) as input and yields versatile monocular (when only I1 is input) or stereo dense maps (d1, d2 ∈Rw×h) related to depth. Inspired by DUSt3R [69], PromptDepth first inputs single or two-view images (I1, I2) into an identical visual encoder, producing visual features, denoted F1, F2 ∈RW ×H×C: F1 = VisEncoder(I1), (1) F2 = VisEncoder(I2). (2) Here, we select DINO [45] as our visual backbone due to its significant robust representation capability. We consider a more straightforward way by exploring an unified dense decoder instead of redundant multi-header architecture, leaving all its task-specific prediction waiting to be activated. For this point, we found that SAM’s prompt engineering [32, 54] potentially performs more versatility than it declared. In particular, we take advantage of its vanilla prompt encoder as our interactive module. This block is responsible for embedding different visual cues, e.g., queried points pi and masks mj, resulting in corresponding discrete Fp and dense representations Fm, respectively: Fp, Fm = PromptEncoder(p, m). (3) We expand its discrete representation to concatenate Fp with a dedicated task token Ft, exclusively indicating the specific task under investigation. This strategy significantly solves the uncertainty of the expected predictions ˆd via a singular decoder. Subsequently, the image features (F1, F2) and the concatenated prompt features [Ft, Fp] are jointly captured by promptDPT. At this step, cascaded Two-Way Transformer equipped with a couple of self- and crossattention to mutual representation: (F ′ t, F ′ p), (F ′ 1, F ′ 2) = PromptDPT([Ft, Fp], (F1, F2)). (4) Here, F ′ t corresponds to the sparse representation of a specified task, and F ′ p refers to the sparse embedding from the queried point. This stage allows sparse and dense embedding to gather features from each other, leading to the most relevant representation. To further leverage this advantage, we regard the final output operation as the dot-product of sparse embedding F ′ t and dense embedding (F ′ 1, F ′ 2) instead of the scratch convolution head of DPT [52].

#### 3.4. Gram Anchoring Regularization

Visual Analysis of Latent Space. Jointly train geometry and instanced representation suffer from conflict learning objectiveness. Where geometric representations preserve textures, but high-level semantics disrupt this detail, resulting in collapse in right part. Based on unified learning, this issue arises from a statistical mismatch between full panoptic depth maps and sparse instances (Fig. 4 middle). Panoptic distributions are broad and multi-modal, while instance distributions are sharp and unimodal. Our ILDS loss addresses this by adapting weight maps for panoptic depth and identifying high-density foreground pixels in sparse maps, isolating them from the background. Gram Anchoring. Recent achievements [62] face comparable challenges with image-level loss and patch-level feature learning conflicts. We similarly address this via gram anchoring for geometric-instance-level training, preserving geometric patch similarity, and fostering robust instance representations. Implemented in a curriculum learning manner [3], we begin with panoptic depth supervision learning solely [76] for geometric representation XG. In subsequent instance-related depth training phrase, the intermediate visual encoder features XS are aligned with XG to maintain these geometric representations. Only similarity between patches are constrained, thus allowing feature to free movement. The Gram loss is calculated as: Lgram = |XT G · XG −XT S · XS|. (9) The final learning objective can be indicated as Ltotal = Lilds + λLgram, where λ is set to 2 following [62].

### 5. Experiment

Training Configuration. Following curriculum learning [3], we train our model in two stages. For the geometric learning stage, the model is trained in a fully supervised learning scheme with panoptic maps only. The second stage serves as interactive finetuning, enabling promptable prediction for instances or stereo tracking. Given a pair of samples, we concurrently gather 8 prompt instance maps, 8 stereoscopic tracking maps, all of which are packed together with monocular and stereo panoptic maps, resulting in 19 maps for one data pair. This behavior saves many reprocessing costs and leads to robust visual representations. We use the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 5e-5. The visual encoder has a reduced rate of 5e-6 to regularization, while the prompt encoder and the cascaded twoway transformer modules operate at 4e-5 to tackle complex interactions. Input images are resized to a short edge of 518 and without other augmentation. All training and inference are conducted on nodes with 4 of 48GB RTX4090 GPU. More details are reported on supplementary. Evaluation & Metrics We evaluate our model’s zero-shot generalization across three tasks without fine-tuning. For monocular depth estimation, we test on KITTI [21], Sintel [6], NYU [15], ETH3D [60], and Diode [65], using threshold accuracy (σ1.25 ↑) and relative error (rel ↓). For corresponding stereo depth in online scenario, we use KITTI, Sintel, and Bonn [46] with the full metric set from [76] (including δ accuracies, AbsRel, and RMSE). Finally, for zero-shot object tracking, we evaluate on DAVIS-17 [51] and YouTube- VOS [73] using the J&F-Mean. This no-finetuning approach allows us to assess the true generalization capabilities of our model and data engine.

### 6. Conclusion & Limitation

We present PromptDepth, an efficient neural network for real-time visual tasks in embodied systems, providing adaptive predictions on a unified dense map for various tasks via prompts. Its lightweight, flexible design allows simple feedforward operation. Trained on large-scale data, it excels in zero-shot tasks like depth estimation, segmentation, and tracking, offering multi-purpose predictions in milliseconds without extra memory, suitable for real-time use. Limitations include sacrificing long-term memory for real-time inference and challenges in key point tracking and dense feature matching for 3D reconstruction. Embodied platforms with intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters keep the model relevant in these scenarios.
