# SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter for Data-Efficient Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Indoor Panoramic Depth Estimation

[Open source PDF](../reference_papers_origin/He_SO3-Equivariant_ViT-Adapter_for_Data-Efficient_Zero-Shot_Sim-to-Real_Indoor_Panoramic_Depth_Estimation_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)

## Abstract

Panoramic depth estimation enables a complete 360◦ understanding of 3D environments but faces significant challenges in generalizing to real-world scenes. While recent zero-shot depth models like Depth Anything achieve remarkable generalization on perspective images, their performance sharply degrades on panoramas due to projection distortions and the lack of spherical geometric awareness. Moreover, collecting large-scale panoramic RGB-D data is costly, hindering the large-scale training of panoramic foundation models. To address these issues, we propose an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter, which transfers the powerful zero-shot capability of the perspective pre-trained ViT to panoramic depth estimation by explicitly incorporating a rotation-equivariant inductive bias. Our adapter introduces an SO(3) deformable cross-attention mechanism to effectively align SO(3)-equivariant features with perspective features, enhancing rotational consistency without modifying the ViT backbone. Trained solely on synthetic panoramas, our framework achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real performance on real indoor benchmarks, including Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D, demonstrating both data efficiency and strong generalization for panoramic depth estimation.

### 1. Introduction

Zero-shot monocular depth estimation aims to predict dense depth maps in unseen scenes from a single RGB image, significantly reducing the cost of collecting depth annotations. For narrow-FoV perspective images, recent zero-shot models such as Marigold [24] and DepthAnything [49,50] have demonstrated remarkable cross-dataset generalization. Compared with their perspective counterparts, panoramic zero-shot depth estimation provides a complete 360° understanding of the environment and effectively reduces blind spots. However, panoramic RGB-D data are far more expensive to collect in real scenes than their perspective counterparts due to the need for specialized hardware and complex calibration [4,43], while vanilla convolution and transformer operators lack rotation-equivariance, leading to structural inconsistency when modeling spherical geometry [9, 17]. These challenges make panoramic zero-shot depth estimation a uniquely underexplored yet practically important problem. The success of zero-shot perspective models provides rich transferable priors for depth estimation, thanks to large-scale pre-training and powerful vision transformer backbones. However, transferring such capability to panoramic images remains non-trivial, as it requires addressing challenges in data availability, spherical geometric modeling, and inference performance simultaneously. Balancing these aspects defines the core challenge in transferring zero-shot depth estimation from perspective to panoramic images, motivating research on data-efficient and geometry-consistent panoramic adaptation. DepthAnywhere [43] adopts a semi-supervised scheme combining labeled real panoramas and pseudo-labeled synthetic data, achieving strong results but at high real data cost. PanDA [4] further removes real depth supervision yet still depends on large-scale real images and a perspective backbone lacking rotation-consistent operators. Despite these advances, existing approaches remain limited in data efficiency, geometric consistency, or inference performance, highlighting the need for a geometryconsistent adaptation framework for zero-shot panoramic depth estimation without real training data. To this end, we propose an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT- Adapter that explicitly introduces operator-level SO(3)equivariance into a frozen perspective ViT. Specifically, we first design an Equivariant Spherical Prior Module (ESPM) based on spherical CNNs [9] to extract multi-scale SO(3)-equivariant features as geometric priors. These features are aligned and fused with ViT features in a rotationequivariant manner via our elaborately developed SO(3) deformable cross-attention mechanism (SO(3)-DCAM), which avoids discretization artifacts while preserving rotationequivariance. Finally, a pair of S2-SO(3) feature interaction component is devised to inject equivariant priors into ViT to improve rotational consistency, while feeding back outputs to enrich the SO(3) features. Our rotation-consistent adaptation framework bridges the geometric discrepancy between perspective and panoramic representations, thereby enabling effective exploitation of the rich depth priors encoded in the pre-trained perspective ViT. This allows our method to leverage its strong sim-to-real generalization capability, achieving strong data efficiency and robust zero-shot panoramic depth estimation. As shown in Fig. 1, in a zero-shot sim-to-real setting, our method seamlessly integrates data efficiency, geometric consistency and inference performance into a unified and transferable framework. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter that brings rotation-equivariant geometric awareness into the frozen perspective ViT, enabling it to extend reasoning capabilities to panoramic images without altering the original architecture. • We propose an SO(3)-DCAM to achieve feature-aligned attention on spheres and rotation groups. It bridges disparate geometric spaces through sparse SO(3) sampling and spherical contextual relative position encoding, ensuring accurate and rotation-consistent feature fusion. • We show that training exclusively on synthetic panoramas enables robust sim-to-real generalization, leading to data-efficient and high-fidelity zero-shot inference on real-world panoramic benchmarks.

### 2. Related Work

Zero-shot Monocular Perspective Depth Estimation. Early monocular depth estimation models pursued zero-shot generalization by jointly training on multiple datasets. To address the performance limitations caused by cross-dataset domain gaps, they adopt affine-invariant losses [3,31,32,53] or explicitly model camera intrinsics [20, 52, 53]. For instance, DistDepth [44] achieves zero-shot generalization under a sim-to-real setting, leveraging structured distillation to transfer depth knowledge from synthetic to real indoor scenes. More recently, DepthAnything [49,50] achieves remarkable zero-shot performance through semi-supervised training on large-scale unlabeled images. Diffusion-based generative approaches [18,24,35,47] fine-tune Stable Diffusion for depth estimation, exploiting its rich visual priors to enhance generalization. Monocular Panoramic Depth Estimation. Monocular panoramic depth estimation methods achieve strong indomain performance. Most existing works focus on mitigating the distortion inherent in equirectangular projection, such as by using geometry-aware convolutions [28, 39], spherical positional encoding [26, 55], projections with less distortion [1, 23, 26, 33, 41, 42], or slice-based projections [30, 37, 54]. With the advances of perspective foundation models, several recent approaches explore zeroshot panoramic depth estimation. 360MonoDepth [33] predicts patchwise tangent depths with MiDaS and fuses them via multi-scale alignment. DepthAnywhere [43] leverages large-scale unlabeled panoramas for semi-supervised distillation, and PanDA [4] further enhances generalization through pseudo-label generation for real-world panoramas and M¨obius augmentation. Although these approaches transfer perspective features to panoramic images, they lack explicit modeling of spherical geometry, limiting the effective use of perspective priors during transfer. Group-equivariant Neural Networks. Groupequivariant neural networks leverage equivariant operations to encode symmetries in data, improving robustness under group transformations while reducing the complexity of both models and samples. Representative approaches to groupequivariant modeling include group convolutions on discrete and continuous transformation groups [8–11,13–17,38,40], which inspire later extensions of equivariance within self-attention mechanisms [19,21]. Our method builds upon Spherical CNNs [9] to extract SO(3)-equivariant spherical features as inputs to the ViT adapter, and is further inspired by LieTransformer [21] in constructing rotation-consistent local neighborhoods within the attention mechanism. Structural and Geometric Enhancements of ViT. Structural enhancements in ViTs improve adaptability and spatial reasoning, such as adapter-based frameworks for efficient transfer [6,7,29] and deformable attention for flexible keypoint sampling [27,46,57]. ### 3. The Proposed Methodology

We propose the SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter, an extension of ViT-Adapter [7] that transfers pre-trained perspective depth knowledge to zero-shot panoramic depth estimation. By introducing a rotation-equivariant inductive bias, our framework empowers the pre-trained ViT to effectively process spherical features for panoramic depth estimation. Specifically, we leverage SO(3)-equivariant features extracted from our ESPM to refine the non-equivariant S2 features produced by the pre-trained ViT on equirectangular panoramas. This rotation-consistent feature injection allows the model to fully exploit the depth priors learned from perspective pre-training, leading to accurate and data-efficient sim-to-real generalization. The overall architecture of our framework is outlined in Sec. 3.1. Sec. 3.2 describes the ESPM for extracting rotation-equivariant SO(3) features. Sec. 3.3 details the SO(3)-DCAM, and Sec. 3.4 presents the S2-SO(3) feature interaction for fusing non-equivariant and equivariant representations.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

As shown in Fig. 2(a), our framework consists of a frozen pre-trained ViT and trainable add-on modules. The architecture involves the following three main steps: Feature Extraction. We take two representations of each panoramic image as input: the equirectangular projection XERP ∈RH×W ×3 and the spherical signal XDH ∈ R2B×2B×3 sampled on the Driscoll–Healy [12] grid with bandwidth B = ⌊H 8 ⌋. For XERP, we divide it into 14 × 14 non-overlapping patches, flatten and project them into Ddimensional tokens via patch embedding, and add positional embeddings to obtain the initial ViT feature F0 vit ∈R HW 142 ×D. For XDH, the ESPM extracts multi-scale SO(3)-equivariant features, which are projected into D dimensions to form the initial equivariant features F0 esp, injected into the pre-trained ViT in the first adapter block. S2-SO(3) Feature Interaction. The Transformer encoder is divided into N = 4 blocks, where each ViT block is augmented with an SO(3) feature injector and a multi-scale feature extractor placed before and after it, respectively. This design aggregates the complementary advantages of pretrained ViT features and SO(3) features within each SO(3)- Equivariant ViT-Adapter block. Decoding. Fi={1,2,...,N} vit from each adapter block are upsampled into multi-resolution representations using 2 × 2 transposed convolutions according to layer depth. Meanwhile, FN esp is projected onto the sphere to obtain FN esp. The

#### 3.2. Equivariant Spherical Prior Module

For perspective images with translation symmetry, using a vanilla CNN-based feature extractor, the spatial local features and translation-equivariant features can be introduced into ViT as inductive biases [7, 22]. However, for panoramic images parameterized by spherical coordinates α ∈[0, 2π), β ∈[0, π], a “translation” in the spherical coordinate grid is essentially a 3D rotation. Thus, we design an Equivariant Spherical Prior Module (ESPM) based on spherical CNNs [9], to provide the frozen ViT with rotationequivariant local spatial contexts on the sphere. We build S2ConvBlock and SO3ConvBlock as basic convolutional units, combining S2Conv and SO3Conv with ReLU activation and residual connections. As shown in Fig. 2(b), given a band-limited spherical signal XDH sampled using the Driscoll–Healy spherical grid, we first lift it to SO(3) via the S2ConvBlock. Then, a series of SO3ConvBlocks are used to extract multi-scale rotation-equivariant features. Finally, linear layers adjust the number of feature channels at each scale to match the embedding dimension in ViT. The resulting features, F1 ∈ RB×B×B×D, F2 ∈ R B 2 × B 2 × B 2 ×D, and F3 ∈ R B 4 × B 4 × B 4 ×D, together form the initial equivariant features F0 esp = {F1, F2, F3}.

#### 3.3. SO(3) Deformable Cross-Attention Module

Most attention mechanisms in computer vision are designed for narrow-FoV perspective images. When applied to spherical representations, they are adversely affected by non-uniform sampling, which distorts global feature matching and leads to suboptimal attention weights. Moreover, they generally rely only on feature similarity, without modeling the relative positional relationships inherent to the spherical manifold. To address these limitations, we propose an SO(3) deformable cross-attention mechanism (SO(3)- DCAM), which generalizes the deformable attention module [57] to the spherical representation by introducing two key innovations: (1) an SO(3) Sparse Sampling for key-value features, which fundamentally replaces the 2D sampling points, and (2) a Spherical Contextual Relative Positional Encoding (SCRPE) that incorporates geometric relationships on the sphere during calculating the attention weights. SO(3) Sparse Sampling. As illustrated in Fig. 2(d), we use perspective features Fvit as queries and sample a small set of key–value features from the equivariant features Fesp. The core of our approach is to lift the sampling operation from 2D Euclidean plane to SO(3) group, which guarantees consistent initial relative positions of neighboring features for any query location zq on S2—a property that mitigates discretization errors from non-uniform spherical sampling. #### 3.4. S2-SO(3) Feature Interaction

When directly applied to ERP images, a ViT pre-trained on narrow-FoV perspective images extracts features that can be regarded as non-equivariant on the sphere S2. While the rich visual priors encoded in the pre-trained model enable it to capture high-quality and generalizable representations, the absence of equivariance renders these features vulnerable to geometric distortions, ultimately limiting the accuracy of depth estimation. To alleviate this limitation, we design two feature interaction modules that bridge the features extracted by ESPM and ViT. Specifically, an SO(3) Feature Injector module injects the equivariant features Fesp on SO(3) into the non-equivariant features Fvit on S2, thereby improving their rotational symmetry. In parallel, a Multi-Scale Feature Extractor module feeds the integrated features back from the ViT to enrich the equivariant features Fesp with depth-aware priors learned from large-scale perspective data. SO(3) Feature Injector. As shown in Fig. 2(c), this injector is implemented with SO(3)-DCAM (Sec. 3.3) and is inserted before each ViT block to progressively inject equivariant cues into the ViT feature stream. At the layer i, the current ViT feature Fi vit serves as query, whereas the SO(3) feature Fi esp, computed after i iterations of the interaction module, serves as key and value. #### 3.5. Loss Function

We employ the BerHu loss [25] to supervise depth regression, which combines the advantages of ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms for stable and robust optimization. It behaves as an ℓ1 loss for small residuals and transitions to an ℓ2 loss for larger errors, effectively balancing accuracy and smoothness. This loss is widely used in depth estimation and provides stable convergence in training our panoramic depth network.

### 5. Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce an SO(3)-Equivariant ViT- Adapter that enhances the rotation consistency in pre-trained perspective ViTs for panoramic depth estimation. Especially, by incorpating our elaborately designed SO(3)-DCAM, our framework aligns features on spheres and rotation groups in a rotation-equivariant manner, enabling effective adaptation using only synthetic panoramic data. Experiments under the zero-shot sim-to-real settings demonstrate that this operator-level innovation provides robust zero-shot generalization to real-world scenes and significantly improves rotation-consistent geometric reasoning for panoramas. We believe this study offers a principled step toward more generalizable and geometry-consistent transformer architectures for spherical vision, thereby advancing the generalization capability of panoramic depth estimation tasks.
